# Link patterns and anchors

## Structure

| Pattern | Rule |
|---|---|
| Hub to spokes | The hub links to every spoke with a short description. |
| Spoke to hub | Every spoke links up to its hub once, early. |
| Spoke to spoke | One or two siblings where the reader would want the next step. |
| Informational to money | Link to the service or product page when the reader is ready to act. |
| Money to proof | Link to the case, guide or policy that answers the buyer's doubt. |

A rule of thumb: three to ten contextual links per 1,000 words, each one useful to the reader.

## Anchors

| Weak | Better |
|---|---|
| click here | how a basic bike service works |
| read more | our brake service prices |
| this page | the cycle safety checklist |

Use 2 to 5 words. Vary wording across pages that link to the same target, and keep the target's main words in most of them.

## Rewriting a sentence

Before: "We service all kinds of bikes."
After: "We service all kinds of bikes, from commuter bikes to e-bikes; see our [basic service](/services/basic-service/)."

Keep the sentence true and in the page's own voice.
